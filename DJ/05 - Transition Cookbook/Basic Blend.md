# Basic Blend

### Technique Name
**The Basic Blend** (Intro-to-Outro Volume Blend)

### What it is
The foundational DJ transition where Track B's intro is introduced during Track A's outro, using channel volume faders and basic crossfader movement over a standard 16 or 32-bar phrase.

### What problem it solves
Provides a smooth, predictable, and unobtrusive passage between two tracks of identical or nearly identical tempo and complementary energy, keeping the dancefloor moving without distraction.

### Musical principle
**Structural Continuity & Phrasing.** By overlapping the percussive outro of Song A with the percussive intro of Song B, the listener perceives a continuous, unbroken drum groove. (See [[Phrasing & Structure]]).

### Setup
* **Track A:** Playing on Deck 1, approaching its outro (last 32 bars).
* **Track B:** Cued on Deck 2 at Beat 1 of its intro (Hot Cue 1).
* **BPM:** Matched exactly (e.g., both at 126.0 BPM).
* **Key:** Within $\pm 1$ Camelot hour (e.g., 8A $\to$ 8A or 9A).
* **Mixer:** Channel 2 Fader at 0. Channel 2 Low EQ at 9 o'clock to avoid initial bass clash.

### Step-by-step
1. **Count the Phrase:** As Track A enters its final 32-bar outro on Bar 1, Beat 1, press **PLAY** on Track B.
2. **Bring up Fader B:** Over the first 8 bars, smoothly raise Track B's channel upfader from 0% to 75%.
3. **Mid-Point Equalization:** At Bar 9 (Beat 33), bring Track B's fader to 100%. Slightly drop Track A's High EQ to make space for Track B's hi-hats.
4. **The Swap:** At Bar 17 (the halfway mark), swap the Low EQs: bring Track B's bass up to 12 o'clock while dialing Track A's bass down to 7 o'clock. (See [[Bass Swap]]).
5. **Fade Out Track A:** Over the final 8–16 bars, smoothly pull Track A's channel fader down from 100% to 0%.

### When to use it
* House, Deep House, Tech House, and Techno sets where hypnotic continuity is the primary aesthetic.
* Warming up a room or during steady-state daytime/club sets.

### When NOT to use it
* High-energy festival EDM, Dubstep, or fast-paced Pop sets where extended drum blends feel sluggish and boring.
* When tracks lack extended percussive intros/outros.

### Best genres
* House, Tech House, Techno, Trance, Melodic Electronic.

### Beginner difficulty
**1 / 5** (The first transition every DJ learns).

### Risk of sounding gimmicky
**0 / 5** (Zero gimmick; this is pure club tradition).

### Common mistakes
* Launching Track B off-phrase (e.g., on bar 3 instead of bar 1).
* Leaving both Low EQs at 12 o'clock, causing muddy low-end distortion.
* Fading Track A out too rapidly, leaving a sudden drop in sonic energy.

### Advanced variation
Layer an energetic high-passed percussion loop from a 3rd deck during the entire 32-bar blend to maintain rhythmic drive. (See [[3-Deck Layering]]).

### Example scenario
Blending from Fisher's *"Losing It"* (outro drums) into Chris Lake's *"Turn Off The Lights"* (intro drums).

### Practice drill
Pick 3 House tracks. Practice executing 32-bar blends between them without touching any effects—only volume faders and Low EQ. Record and ensure there is zero perceived volume dip.
