# Reverb Transition

### Technique Name
**The Reverb Transition** (Spatial Wash / Reverb Freeze & Fade)

### What it is
A transition where a large hall or arena Reverb (often with a high-pass filter) is applied to Track A, diffusing its rhythmic transients into an expansive ambient cloud, allowing Track B to emerge cleanly from the atmospheric fog.

### What problem it solves
Solves rhythmic clashes when transitioning between songs with completely incompatible drum patterns, syncopation styles, or swung grooves (e.g., a straight 4/4 four-on-the-floor beat into a broken, syncopated breakbeat or trap rhythm).

### Musical principle
**Diffusion & Dynamic Reset.** Reverb smears discrete percussive impulses across hundreds of micro-reflections, effectively removing the rhythm while preserving the emotional harmonic pad. This cleanses the listener's rhythmic palate. (See [[Effects (FX) Mastery]]).

### Setup
* **Mixer FX:** Beat FX assigned to Channel 1. FX: **REVERB**.
* **Filter:** Low EQ turned down to 8 o'clock (or HPF turned to 11 o'clock) to prevent low-end mud.
* **Track B:** Cued on a melodic intro, vocal pickup, or beat 1.

### Step-by-step
1. **The Lead-Up:** Identify a melodic breakdown, vocal phrase, or the final 4 bars of Track A's chorus.
2. **Engage the Reverb Wash:** At Bar 8, engage the Reverb with Wet/Dry at 60%–80%.
3. **Cut the Bass:** Simultaneously kill Track A's Low EQ.
4. **The Fade:** As Track A turns into a lush, floating celestial wash, slowly lower its channel upfader over 4 to 8 bars.
5. **The Arrival:** Start Track B. Because Track A has no rhythmic kick, Track B's drums enter with crystalline clarity while the reverb cloud of Track A slowly evaporates in the background.

### When to use it
* Emotional electronic music, Melodic House, Future Bass, ambient pop transitions.
* Transitioning out of an emotional ballad into a dance groove.
* Highly favored by artists like [[Fred again.. Case Study]] to transition between nostalgic voice notes and energetic beats.

### When NOT to use it
* Fast, aggressive Dubstep, Hard Techno, or Drum & Bass where sharpness and aggression are required.
* Never use reverb over an active kick drum without low-cut filtering.

### Best genres
* Melodic Electronic, Indie Dance, Future Bass, Pop, Organic House.

### Beginner difficulty
**2 / 5** (Easy to trigger, but requires discipline to cut the low-end).

### Risk of sounding gimmicky
**1 / 5** (Sounds lush and cinematic when used appropriately).

### Common mistakes
* Forgetting to cut the bass EQ, creating a massive, booming mud-cloud that overloads the subwoofer amplifiers.
* Letting the reverb wash linger for too long (more than 16 bars), causing the dancefloor to lose its forward momentum.

### Advanced variation
The **Reverb Freeze Drop:** On hardware with an FX Freeze button (or using Pioneer DJM-V10 / Ableton), hit `FREEZE` on a specific vocal word. The vocal syllable becomes an infinite, shimmering synth pad. You can then change the master tempo at will while the pad sustains, dropping the next song at any BPM.

### Example scenario
Transitioning from Fred again..'s *"Adore U"* into a melodic Bicep anthem.

### Practice drill
Take a pop vocal track. Engage a high-passed reverb on the final word of the chorus, cut the channel fader, and bring in a clean house kick drum underneath the fading tail.
