# Backspin (Spinback)

### Technique Name
**The Backspin** (The Spinback / Rewind / Vinyl Wheel Rip)

### What it is
A physical, high-energy transition technique where the DJ forcefully spins the jog wheel or vinyl platter backward with their hand, producing a characteristic high-pitched, accelerating rewinding scream (`whirrrrrr-zip!`), immediately followed by cutting the fader and dropping Track B on the downbeat.

### What problem it solves
Solves the problem of ending a song with maximum authority when no smooth musical transition is possible or desired. It serves as an unmistakable, intentional auditory full-stop that resets the room's attention.

### Musical principle
**Psychoacoustic White-Noise Wash & Cultural Convention.** The high-velocity reverse playback blurs pitch and rhythm into a broadband white-noise frequency sweep. In club, dancehall, and UK sound system culture, the rewind/spinback is a deeply ingrained symbol of celebration and track reset. (See [[Rules vs Principles]]).

### Setup
* **Vinyl Mode:** **MANDATORY (ON)** on Deck 1.
* **Vinyl Speed Adjust / Touch Sensitivity:** Calibrated to allow a smooth, frictionless spinback.
* **Track B:** Cued on Deck 2 at Beat 1 of its drop, verse, or iconic vocal hook.

### Step-by-step
1. **The Lead-Up:** Ride Track A until the final bar of the chorus or phrase (Bar 8 or 16).
2. **The Spin (Beat 3 or Beat 4):**
   * Place your palm or fingers firmly on the top platter of Deck 1's jog wheel.
   * **Flick your wrist counter-clockwise with high velocity**, sending the platter into a rapid backward spin.
3. **The Cut:** As the spinback reaches its highest screeching pitch (on Beat 4):
   * **Slam Channel 1 fader to 0 (or snap crossfader).**
   * *Optional:* Engage a 1/2-beat echo to let the tail of the spinback ring out into space.
4. **The Drop (Beat 1):** On the exact downbeat of the next measure, hit **PLAY** on Track B at 100% volume!

### When to use it
* UK Garage, Drum & Bass, Dubstep, Bass House, Hip-Hop.
* When an unreleased "ID" or massive crowd favorite gets such an overwhelming reaction that you "pull it back" or slam into an even bigger anthem.
* Heavily used by [[Skrillex Case Study]] and UK club DJs.

### When NOT to use it
* Deep House, Minimal Techno, or Ambient sets (sounds absurd and destructive to a subtle groove).
* More than 2 or 3 times in an entire 2-hour set (overuse makes the DJ look desperate and lazy).

### Best genres
* Drum & Bass, UK Bassline, Dubstep, Hip-Hop, Grime.

### Beginner difficulty
**2 / 5** (Simple to perform physically; requires crisp timing to hit Beat 1).

### Risk of sounding gimmicky
**3 / 5** (If overused, it sounds like an amateur who doesn't know how to mix).

### Common mistakes
* Spinback is too weak (dies after half a second with a sad thud).
* Spinback is too long (spins for 3 bars, killing all dancefloor momentum).
* Doing it with Vinyl Mode turned OFF (the CDJ will simply nudge instead of spinning backward!).

### Advanced variation
The **Echoed High-Pass Spinback:** Engage a High-Pass Filter and a 1-beat Echo on Track A right as you spin back. The spinback floats upward into the reverb sky while you drop Track B underneath it.

### Example scenario
Spinning back a heavy Skrillex dubstep drop to instantly cut into a classic 174 BPM Chase & Status drum & bass roller.

### Practice drill
Enable Vinyl Mode. Practice flicking the wheel backward on Beat 4 and hitting Hot Cue 1 of Track B on the subsequent downbeat. Time it so the spinback sound lasts exactly 1 beat.
