# Echo Out

### Technique Name
**The Echo Out** (Delay Freeze / Post-Fader Echo Throw)

### What it is
A transition where a tempo-synced Echo or Delay effect (typically 1/2 beat, 3/4 beat, or 1 beat) is applied to Track A on the final beat of a phrase, and the channel fader is immediately pulled to zero, allowing the repeating echo tail to decay naturally into space while Track B is dropped on the downbeat.

### What problem it solves
The ultimate Swiss Army knife transition for open-format DJs. Solves the problem of transitioning between two songs with incompatible keys, wildly different BPMs (e.g., 128 BPM to 174 BPM), or completely disparate genres.

### Musical principle
**Acoustic Masking & Spatial Dissolution.** The rhythmic decay of the echo fills the acoustic void, maintaining the illusion of rhythm while the original musical pitch and bass disappear, resetting the listener's ear for an entirely new tempo or tonal center. (See [[Effects (FX) Mastery]]).

### Setup
* **Mixer FX:** Beat FX assigned to Channel 1 (or Master). FX type: **ECHO**.
* **Beats Parameter:** Set to **1/2 beat** (for energetic snap) or **3/4 beat** (for syncopated reggae/dub groove) or **1 beat**.
* **Dry/Wet:** Set to 50%–70%.
* **Track B:** Cued on Beat 1 of its intro, verse, or drop.

### Step-by-step
1. **The Lead-Up:** Let Track A play until the final bar of an 8 or 16-bar phrase (Bar 8 or Bar 16).
2. **The Throw (Beat 4):** On Beat 4 of that final bar (or on the final spoken vocal syllable):
   * **Hit the FX ON button.**
   * **Instantly pull Track A's channel fader down to 0.**
3. **The Decay:** Because modern DJ mixers feature **Post-Fader FX**, the audio continues echoing in the room even though the channel fader is closed.
4. **The Impact (Beat 1):** On the exact downbeat of the next bar, slam Track B's fader to 100% and hit **PLAY** (or trigger Hot Cue 1).

### When to use it
* Radical genre transitions (e.g., Pop to Drum & Bass, Hip-Hop to House).
* Changing tempo dramatically (e.g., jumping from 128 BPM to 174 BPM or 100 BPM to 128 BPM).
* Ending a track abruptly after a massive sing-along vocal chorus.

### When NOT to use it
* Deep House or Techno sets where long, seamless continuity is expected (doing an echo out every 2 minutes sounds lazy and amateur).
* When both songs can be blended harmonically and rhythmically.

### Best genres
* Open-Format, Pop, Hip-Hop, Drum & Bass, Dubstep, Festival EDM.

### Beginner difficulty
**2 / 5** (Simple to execute, but requires crisp physical timing on Beat 4).

### Risk of sounding gimmicky
**2 / 5** (High risk if used on every song; low risk when reserved for major structural genre jumps).

### Common mistakes
* Using Pre-Fader FX instead of Post-Fader FX (which cuts the echo dead when you pull the fader down).
* Echoing out on an active sub-bass kick drum, causing a booming, muddy echo rumble. (Always kill the Low EQ right before hitting the echo!).
* Missing the downbeat drop of Track B, leaving dead silence.

### Advanced variation
The **High-Pass Echo Freeze:** Sweep the High-Pass Filter up while simultaneously engaging a 1-beat echo with 90% feedback, capturing an ethereal vocal shimmer that rings out for 16 bars while you blend the next track underneath.

### Example scenario
Mixing The Weeknd's *"Blinding Lights"* (171 BPM Pop) into Sub Focus's *"Desire"* (174 BPM Drum & Bass), or jumping from Fisher (126 BPM) to Drake (85 BPM).

### Practice drill
Set your Beat FX to Echo at 1/2 beat. Practice playing a track, counting `1, 2, 3, 4`, and throwing the fader down on Beat 4 so the echo rings out cleanly. Drop Track B on Beat 1. Repeat 10 times until muscle memory is 100% reliable.
