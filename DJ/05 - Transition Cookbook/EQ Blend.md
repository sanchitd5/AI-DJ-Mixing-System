# EQ Blend

### Technique Name
**The EQ Blend** (Frequency Replacement Mixing)

### What it is
A transition executed primarily by manipulating the 3-band EQ knobs (High, Mid, Low) rather than channel faders, surgically replacing the frequencies of Track A with those of Track B across 16 to 32 bars.

### What problem it solves
Prevents the perceived volume drop or muddy accumulation that occurs when simply pushing two volume faders up and down.

### Musical principle
**Frequency Carving & Headroom Preservation.** By treating the audio spectrum as three separate shelves ($<250\text{ Hz}$, $250\text{--}5\text{ kHz}$, $>5\text{ kHz}$), you ensure the overall summing bus stays within $0\text{--}+2\text{ dB}$ of headroom while elements are traded. (See [[EQ & Frequency Management]]).

### Setup
* **Channel Faders:** Bring Channel 2's fader up to 100% *before* starting the audible transition, but with all 3 EQ knobs turned down (High at 8 o'clock, Mid at 7 o'clock, Low at 7 o'clock).
* **Track A:** All EQs at 12 o'clock.

### Step-by-step
1. **Bars 1–8 (The Top-End Swap):** Smoothly bring Track B's High EQ from 8 o'clock to 12 o'clock, while slightly bringing Track A's High EQ back to 10 o'clock. Track B's percussion is now audible.
2. **Bars 9–16 (The Mid-Range Swap):** Gradually bring Track B's Mid EQ up to 12 o'clock while reducing Track A's Mid EQ to 8 o'clock. The musical instrumentation and chords have now transferred.
3. **Bar 17, Beat 1 (The Bass Swap):** On the downbeat of the new phrase, snap Track B's Low EQ to 12 o'clock and Track A's Low EQ to 7 o'clock. (See [[Bass Swap]]).
4. **Bars 17–24 (The Departure):** Pull Track A's remaining High and Mid EQs to zero, then drop Track A's channel fader to 0.

### When to use it
* Deep House, Tech House, Nu-Disco, Melodic EDM.
* Any time two tracks have high energy and you want an undetectable, transparent handoff.

### When NOT to use it
* Fast-paced Hip-Hop, Trap, or Dubstep where sudden dynamic contrast is required.
* Songs with complex vocal hooks that cannot be isolated with standard EQ.

### Best genres
* House, Techno, Melodic Electronic, Afro House.

### Beginner difficulty
**2 / 5** (Requires two hands coordinating opposite rotary motions on EQ knobs).

### Risk of sounding gimmicky
**0 / 5** (The gold standard of professional club mixing).

### Common mistakes
* Turning up Track B's EQs without attenuating Track A, causing the master output to clip in the red.
* Swapping the bass off-beat (e.g., on beat 2 or 3 instead of the downbeat).

### Advanced variation
Combine with subtle Color FX Filter movement on the outgoing channel to round off the sharp edges during the mid-range swap.

### Example scenario
Mixing between Dom Dolla's *"Rhyme Dust"* and John Summit's *"Where You Are"* in a peak-time club setting.

### Practice drill
Put your hands on the EQ knobs: Left hand on Track A Low EQ, Right hand on Track B Low EQ. Practice executing an instantaneous, simultaneous 180-degree turn precisely on the downbeat of bar 16.
