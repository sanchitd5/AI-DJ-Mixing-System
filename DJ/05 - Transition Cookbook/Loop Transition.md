# Loop Transition

### Technique Name
**The Loop Transition** (Infinite Groove / Structural Extension Mix)

### What it is
A transition where an auto-loop (typically 4, 8, or 16 bars) is engaged on Track A's percussive outro or groove, repeating it for at most two cycles, which buys the DJ enough time to layer, EQ-blend, and introduce Track B without the groove going stale.

### What problem it solves
Solves the problem of short, rushed outros. Many modern streaming edits fade out or end within 4 to 8 bars, leaving no time for a professional mix. Looping creates an artificial, customized DJ-extended mix on the fly.

### Musical principle
**Hypnotic Ostinato & Temporal Extension.** Repeating a static rhythmic pattern conditions the brain to treat it as an ambient rhythmic texture, reducing its psychological prominence and clearing cognitive space for new melodic information from Track B. (See [[Loops & Beat Jumps]]).

### Setup
* **Track A:** Playing on Deck 1.
* **Auto-Loop:** Set to **8 bars (32 beats)** or **4 bars (16 beats)** with Quantize ON.
* **Track B:** Cued on Deck 2 at intro downbeat (Hot Cue 1).

### Step-by-step
1. **Engage the Loop (2 cycles max):** Loop A's last clean 8-bar groove at most 2 cycles, then hand off; never hold it open-ended. Hit **Auto-Loop** on Beat 1 of the phrase as Track A enters its final clean groove section.
2. **Launch Track B:** When ready, launch Track B on Beat 1 of Track A's loop cycle.
3. **The Blend:** Use standard [[EQ Blend]] techniques across the two loop cycles (16 bars for an 8-bar loop) to introduce Track B's percussion and mids.
4. **Bass Swap:** On Beat 1 of Track B's main phrase, execute the [[Bass Swap]].
5. **Exit:** By the end of the second cycle, disengage the loop or fade Track A's upfader down to 0 smoothly. If Track B still is not ready, leave with an [[Echo Out]] instead of looping again.

### When to use it
* Mixing short radio edits or tracks without extended DJ intros/outros.
* When you need extra time to read the crowd, find the next track, or troubleshoot gear.
* Tech House, Minimal, Deep House, and Disco.

### When NOT to use it
* Looping a section with repetitive, annoying vocal phrases (looping a vocal for 48 bars will drive the crowd insane).
* Looping an uneven phrase length (e.g., 3 bars or 6 bars) which breaks the 8-bar dancefloor phrasing.

### Best genres
* Tech House, Deep House, Melodic Techno, Nu-Disco, Pop edits.

### Beginner difficulty
**2 / 5** (Simple once Quantize is active).

### Risk of sounding gimmicky
**0 / 5** (Essential utility skill used in every professional DJ booth).

### Common mistakes
* Looping a bar with an unfinished vocal word or chopped cymbal tail, creating an awkward click on every cycle.
* Leaving the loop running for 3 minutes without introducing the next song, boring the audience.

### Advanced variation
The **Loop Halving Filter Out:** As Track B begins playing, cut Track A's loop in half every 4 bars ($8 \text{ bars} \to 4 \text{ bars} \to 2 \text{ bars} \to 1 \text{ bar} \to 1/2 \text{ beat}$) while sweeping a High-Pass Filter, turning Track A into a synthetic build-up before cutting it dead.

### Example scenario
Mixing out of a radio edit of Dua Lipa (which ends abruptly) by looping 4 bars of clean bass groove before the track fades out.

### Practice drill
Take 5 radio edits with no DJ outros. Practice catching an 8-bar loop on each one and executing a seamless 32-bar blend into an incoming track.
