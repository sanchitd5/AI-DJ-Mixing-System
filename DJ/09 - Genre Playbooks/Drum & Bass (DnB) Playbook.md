# Drum & Bass (DnB) DJ Playbook: High-Velocity Kinetic Energy

Drum & Bass (DnB) is the highest-velocity mainstream electronic dance music genre. Characterized by lightning-fast tempos, complex breakbeat syncopation, and devastating sub-bass design, DnB DJing demands the quickest ears, fastest reflexes, and most disciplined phrasing in all of DJing.

---

## 1. Genre Profile & Technical Specifications

* **Typical BPM:** 170–176 BPM (Club Standard: **174.0 BPM**).
* **Rhythmic Characteristics:** Fast, syncopated breakbeat pattern: Kick on Beat 1, Snare on Beat 2, subtle syncopated Kick on Beat 3-and, Snare on Beat 4. Characterized by high kinetic energy, rolling ghost snares, and heavy 808 sub-bass or oscillating Reese basslines.
* **Sub-Genres to Know:**
  * **Dancefloor DnB (Sub Focus, Dimension, Wilkinson):** Melodic, bright supersaw leads, massive vocal anthems, stadium festival appeal.
  * **Jump-Up (Hedex, Bou):** Raw, squeaky, metallic synth riffs, heavy bass stabs, high-energy party atmosphere.
  * **Liquid DnB (Hybrid Minds, Calibre):** Soulful vocals, warm Rhodes pianos, rolling liquid sub-bass; emotional and introspective.
  * **Neurofunk (Noisia, Black Sun Empire):** Dark, aggressive, technical cyberpunk bass design.
* **Common Structures:** 32-bar or 64-bar phrases; 16-bar fast build-ups; drops lasting 32 or 64 bars.
* **Common Transition Points:** Rapid cuts and swaps occurring every 32 to 64 bars (DnB DJs mix at 2x to 3x the speed of House DJs!).
* **Typical Energy Curves:** Constant high-voltage kinetic drive (Energy 7–10).

---

## 2. Mixing Challenges & Tactical Solutions

### Challenge 1: The High-Velocity Trainwreck
* **Problem:** At 174 BPM, a 50-millisecond phase error doesn't sound like a slight nudge—it sounds like an explosion of falling pots and pans.
* **Tactical Solution:** Master transient alignment on the snare drum. In your headphones, listen to the crack of the snare on Beat 2. Nudge the outer jog wheel bezel until both snares snap as a single gunshot.

### Challenge 2: Phrasing at High Speed
* **Problem:** Because the music moves so quickly (174 BPM = ~2.9 beats per second), an 8-bar phrase passes in just 11 seconds!
* **Tactical Solution:** Plan cue points obsessively. Use **Hot Cues** to launch builds and drops without manual needle scrolling.

## 3. Core Techniques & Transition Playbook

* **Useful Techniques:** [[Double Drop]], [[Drop Swap]], [[Backspin (Spinback)]], [[Quick Cut]], [[Acapella Overlay]].
* **Common Mistakes:** Allowing both sub-basses to play simultaneously (will immediately redline the club mixer); letting tracks ride together for too long without EQ cuts.

### How DnB DJs Transition INTO Drum & Bass:
* **From Hip-Hop / R&B (87 BPM):** Mathematical double-time lock ($87 \times 2 = 174$). Layer DnB breakbeats over the rap verse and drop into DnB on the chorus. (See [[Genre Bridge Playbook]]).
* **From Pop / House (124–128 BPM):** Take an emotional vocal breakdown; bring in high-speed 174 BPM shakers; drop into a vocal Dancefloor DnB anthem.

### How DnB DJs Transition OUT of Drum & Bass:
* **First:** another DnB track, [[Bass Swap]] on the phrase.
* **Into Pop / R&B (120 BPM):** Breakdown first, [[Echo Out]] second: wait for the final breakdown, reverb-wash and echo out the last phrase, then let the pop vocal enter out of the ambient space. No tempo ramp.
* **Into Dubstep (140 BPM):** Breakdown first, [[Echo Out]] second: echo out the last snare of the breakdown and start the dubstep on Beat 1 of its phrase. No tempo ramp.
